## A) Submission description (Contra / main entry)

**ekam — many hands, one canvas**

We spend all day together online. The same feeds, the same clips — together, but never quite *with*
each other. We consume together, constantly. We almost never *make* anything together.

So I built a place where strangers could. **ekam** — Sanskrit for *one* — is a single shared canvas
of 576 little squares. You claim one tile, hand-paint it in your browser, and leave one line about
why. Zoom out and it's a quilt of hundreds of strangers; zoom in and every square is one person's
hand — their painting, their name, their story. A published tile is permanent; nobody paints over
you. Where r/place was a battlefield, this is a quilt: **care instead of chaos.**

No login, no account, no noise — you tap an open tile and it's instantly yours. A real little studio
opens on your blank square: brushes, colours, fills, your own hand. *Leave the words. Draw the lines.
Say what's in your mind.* And a **dusty lofi radio** plays the whole time — twenty tracks, drifting,
following you across every page while you paint. Warm embers rise behind everything; a trail of light
follows your cursor. The chrome stays quiet on purpose, so the only real colour on screen is what
people painted.

The hardest part of a public wall painted by strangers is that someone, somewhere, will try to slip
something in. So every tile is screened by an **AI moderator (Claude Opus 4.8) in seconds** — two
adversarial passes, in any language — catching the hateful or political while leaving real art
completely free. The machine is fast, but a human always has the last word. And when the canvas
closes, it reveals itself: one seamless artwork, every empty tile rendered as paper, a scrolling reel
of everyone who made it, downloadable forever.

**How it was built — and how you could too.** It started in **Figma**: the design system, the type
ramp, every screen. Then the **Figma MCP** became the bridge — the *same* agent (Claude Code) read
and wrote the **Figma file *and* the production app** through one pipeline, so design and code never
drifted apart. Next.js + Supabase run the live wall on Vercel; the Claude API is the moderator; and
the film was finished in **Figma Weave**. Design and code, held by the same hands.

576 strangers. One canvas. One small, permanent moment in history. **Come leave your mark.**
